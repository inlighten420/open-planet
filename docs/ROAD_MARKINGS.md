# Road markings: South African rules for our algorithm

Date: 2026-10-12. Scope: what to paint, where, and why, for SA roads.

## Read this first

- **The rules come from three documents.** All are free on the SARTSMA site. They are dated May 2012. SA uses the SADC manual as its national manual.
  - **[V1]** SADC Road Traffic Signs Manual, Vol 1, Chapter 7 "Road Markings". This has the meaning, colour, size and warrants of each marking.
    https://www.sartsma.co.za/_files/ugd/4f7773_c4e3f39ec22848b08d31fff846ce53ed.pdf
  - **[V2]** SARTSM Vol 2, Chapter 2 "Road Marking Applications". This shows how to combine markings on real roads. It also holds the no-overtaking warrant figures and Table 2.3 (all sizes).
    https://www.sartsma.co.za/_files/ugd/4f7773_19b47cc1263549bbb1e13e4e0a583295.pdf
  - **[V4]** SADC RTSM Vol 4, Chapter 12 "Road Markings". This has the full dimensions. See Table 12.1.
    https://www.sartsma.co.za/_files/ugd/4f7773_31778787fa994114bd4100de385f084f.pdf
  - Index of all chapters: https://www.sartsma.co.za/saroadsignsmanuals
  - **[NRTR]** National Road Traffic Regulations, 2000 (R.225). Reg 285 says road markings are "Class II" signs, listed in Schedule 1. Reg 298A covers driving on the shoulder.
    https://source.acts.co.za/national-road-traffic-act-1996/r225_285__purpose__classificat.php
    https://source.acts.co.za/national-road-traffic-act-1996/r225_298a__prohibition_on_driv.php
- **Note:** SARTSM Vol 2 Chapter 7 is about railway crossings, not overtaking. The no-overtaking warrant is in [V1] 7.2.5 and [V2] 2.2.3.
- **Old names:** "Centre line" and "barrier line" are no longer used. The new names are DIVIDING LINE (WM3, broken), NO OVERTAKING LINE (RM1, one solid line) and NO CROSSING LINE (RM2, two solid lines). Source: [V1] 7.2.5.2, Fig 7.3 note 1.
- **Code letters:** RTM = regulatory transverse. RM = regulatory. WM = warning. GM = guidance.
- **Units:** widths are in mm. Lengths and gaps are in m.
- **Legal minimum:** the minimum width of any line is 100 mm ([V1] 7.1.5.1). Wider is allowed. Freeways normally use wider lines ([V1] 7.1.13).

**Modules ([V1] 7.1.5; [V2] 2.5.1.5):**
- A rural module is 12 m. An urban module is 9 m.
- **Freeways always use 12 m modules**, in towns too.
- The rural module may be used on urban roads at 80 km/h or more. The urban module may be used on rural roads under 80 km/h ([V1] 7.4.1.8, 7.3.3.2).
- On multi-lane roads, line up the dashes across the road ([V1] Fig 7.2). On curves, set the modules out on the dividing line.

**Placement ([V1] 7.1.6):**
- Keep lines at least 150 mm from the edge of the surfacing. Use 250 mm if studs go between.
- Parallel lines must be at least 50 mm apart. Use 150 mm if studs go between.

**Tapers ([V2] Table 2.1 = [V1] Table 7.3):** these are for shifting a line with no kerbed island.

| Speed (km/h) | 30 | 50 | 60 | 80 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taper, 1 in ... | 20 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 50 |

- The tapers in front of kerbed islands are flatter. They depend on island width; see the table itself.
- Round taper lengths to whole modules ([V1] 7.1.10.5).

### 1d. Road studs ([V1] 7.5.2; [V2] 2.2.6, Table 2.2)

**Colours:**
- **Red** means prohibition. It goes with RM1, RM2 and RM4.2, and with wrong-way warnings.
- **Yellow** means warning. It goes with yellow lines.
- **White** means guidance. It goes with white lines.

**Use:** studs go on all freeways ([V2] 2.2.6.3(f)).

**Spacing, centre to centre (m):**

| | Normal | Intermediate | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rural | 24 | 12 | 6 |
| Urban | 18 | 9 | 3 |
| Temporary | 12 | 6 | 3 down to 1 |

## 2. Yellow vs white in SA

| Colour | Used for | Source |
|---|---|---|
| White | Dividing line, no-overtaking and no-crossing lines, lane lines, continuity lines, channelising lines, the **right** edge line, stop, yield and pedestrian lines, most arrows | [V1] each subsection |
| Yellow | **Left** edge line (RM4.1), painted-island bars, mandatory direction arrows (RM8), no-parking lines, exclusive lane lines (RM9), box junction | [V1] 7.2.8, 7.2.9, 7.2.12; [V4] Table 12.1 |
| Red | No-stopping lines; red studs | [V1] 7.2.16, 7.5.2 |

**Edge lines on rural roads:**
- The left edge line is always yellow. It is warranted on any road, rural or urban, that has a surfaced shoulder. It may also be used where vehicles damage the road edge ([V1] 7.1.9.3(a)).
- Don't create a shoulder with an edge line where slow vehicles would just drive on it. Mark an extra lane instead ([V1] 7.2.8.11).
- Opposite a side road, taper the edge line towards the road edge. Then cars can pass a right-turner on the left without crossing the line ([V1] 7.2.8.9).

**Edge lines on urban roads:**
- They are allowed, but they are uncommon, except on dual carriageways.
- Kerbed urban streets usually get red no-stopping or yellow no-parking lines along the kerb instead ([V2] 2.4.4.4).
- A right edge line is not needed next to urban medians with a barrier or kerbs that cars can't mount ([V1] 7.2.8.6).

**"Yellow line driving" (moving onto the shoulder to let someone pass)** ([NRTR] reg 298A):
- Driving on the shoulder is banned, with one exception. You may use it only when **all** of these are true:
  - the road has **one lane each way**;
  - it is between sunrise and sunset;
  - you are being overtaken;
  - it is safe;
  - you can see people and vehicles at least 150 m away.
- This affects AI traffic, not the paint.

## 3. No-overtaking lines (the warrant)

**How to measure ([V1] 7.2.5.5; [V2] 2.2.3.15, Fig 2.15, Fig 2.16):**
- Eye height is **1.05 m**. Object height is **1.30 m** (a vehicle).
- The sight distance used is the **Barrier Sight Distance (BSD)**. It is about twice the stopping sight distance. It lets two cars heading at each other both stop.
- Check vertical and horizontal curves together. Check **each direction separately**.
- On horizontal curves, the sight line may not pass outside the shoulder break point. This allows for bushes.
- Method (Fig 2.15): slide a "stringline" of length BSD along the road. Where a driver at 1.05 m cannot see a 1.30 m object at the far end, that direction has a "limited sight zone". Mark an RM1 for that direction over the zone.

**Warrant table ([V1] Table 7.4; [V2] Fig 2.15):** mark RM1 where the available sight distance is less than this.

| Design speed (km/h) | 50 | 60 | 80 | 100 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum BSD (m) | 150 | 180 | 250 | 300 | 400 |

> **Unconfirmed:** the SA manual uses BSD, not the US-style "passing sight distance". I could not find an SA passing-sight-distance table (TRH17 or the SANRAL guide) online. Use BSD; it is the SA warrant.

**Minimum lengths ([V1] Table 7.5; [V2] Fig 2.15, 2.3.5.3, 2.4.5.3):**

| Situation | Legal minimum (m) | Recommended minimum (m) |
|---|---|---|
| Between junctions, rolling terrain or curving road | – | **150** |
| Between junctions, mountainous terrain | – | **60** |
| Rural junction, main road approach | 12 | 24 (uncontrolled). **60 per approach** in the [V2] junction details. |
| Rural junction, STOP or YIELD side-road approach | 12 | 24 to 60. Longer at higher speed. |
| Urban junction, signal or uncontrolled approach | 9 | 18, or 27 on multi-lane roads |
| Urban junction, STOP or YIELD approach | 9 | 18. 27–45 m on the main road ([V2] 2.4.5.3). |

- All lengths are whole modules ([V1] Table 7.5 note 2).

**Gaps and joining:**
- A gap between two RM1 or RM2 sections should be **at least 120 m**. This applies in the same or the opposite direction. If the gap would be shorter, join the sections ([V1] 7.2.5.9(c); [V2] Fig 2.16 note 2).
- At a junction, break the main-road RM1 over the side road:
  - **rural**: 20 m, as gap 8, line 4, gap 8;
  - **urban**: 15 m, as gap 6, line 3, gap 6.
  Centre the break on the side road ([V2] 2.3.5.3, 2.4.5.3).

**Warning before a no-overtaking line starts (WM8) ([V1] 7.3.8; [V2] 2.3.4.7):**
- Put arrows on the dividing-line dashes before RM1 or RM2 starts. Use 2, preferably 3.
- Rural: at **24, 60 and 108 m** before the start. Urban: at **18, 45 and 81 m**.
- Each arrow is as long as a dividing-line dash: 4 m rural, 3 m urban.
- There is no special "end" marking. The solid line simply stops and the broken line carries on.

> **Unconfirmed:** whether the RM1 should run past the end of the limited sight zone. Fig 2.15 shows it covering the zone only. No extra length is stated.

**One direction or both: the three systems ([V2] 2.2.3.14, 2.3.4.6, Fig 2.14):**

| System | One direction banned | Both directions banned |
|---|---|---|
| **1-line** | Can't do it | One RM1 replaces the WM3 |
| **2-line ("hybrid")** | RM1 beside the WM3, on the banned side | One RM1 replaces the WM3 (this is not a no-crossing line) |
| **3-line** | RM1 beside the WM3, on the banned side | RM1 on both sides of the WM3. This makes an RM2 in effect. |

- The solid line is always on the side of the traffic it bans. RM1 goes "to the left of" the WM3, seen from the banned direction.
- If some places will ban only one direction, use the 2-line or 3-line system ([V2] 2.3.4.6).
- Use RM2 (true no-crossing) only where turning across must also be banned ([V1] 7.2.6.3).

> **Unconfirmed:** which system the N2 and Western Cape roads use. Photos show broken+solid pairs, which fits the 2-line or 3-line system. **Default: the 3-line system.** Check it on photos.

## 4. Junctions

**Which road gets the markings:**
- The minor road (the one with the STOP or YIELD sign) gets the stop or yield line. Stop lines only go where there is a STOP sign or signals. Yield lines only go where there is a YIELD sign ([V1] 7.1.9.3(g), 7.2.1.3, 7.2.2.3).
- The main road gets an RM1 on each approach, broken over the side road (see section 3) ([V2] 2.3.5.3).
- On the side road, the dividing line next to the stop or yield line becomes RM1 (or RM2). It must meet the Table 7.5 minimum: 12 m rural, 9 m urban ([V1] 7.2.1.6, 7.2.2.5; [V2] Fig 2.13).

**Stop or yield? A sight-distance test ([V2] Fig 2.13, Detail 2.13.1):**
- The side-road driver sits **20 m** back from the main-road edge (for yield) or **5 m** back (for stop). That driver must see a vehicle at distance S along the main road, both ways.
- S depends on the main road's speed and the design vehicle: P = passenger car; SU+T = truck with trailer.

| Main road speed (km/h) | 60 | 80 | 100 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yield, S for P (m) | 95 | 130 | 155 | 190 |
| Yield, S for SU+T (m) | 140 | 185 | 230 | 280 |
| Stop, S for P, W = 7.5 m (m) | 120 | 160 | 195 | 235 |
| Stop, S for SU+T, W = 15 m (m) | 250 | 330 | 420 | 500 |

- If the yield sight is not available, use STOP.
- The eye and object heights for this test are not given in the figure (**unconfirmed**). Suggest 1.05 m and 1.30 m, as in section 3.

**Position ([V1] 7.1.5.4, 7.2.1.7, 7.2.2.8):**
- Place the lines by the edge of the main road, not by the road reserve.
- Stop line: **1.2 m to 15 m** back from the line of the crossing road's edge.
- Yield line: at least 1.2 m back.
- On a slip road, place the yield line where drivers can see the traffic they must yield to.

**Multi-lane approaches ([V1] 7.4.1.5, 7.2.7.3):**
- Use a "stacking lane": a solid RM3 between lanes, **12 m** long rural or **9 m** urban, up to the stop line.
- Before it, use extra-density GM1 (2/2 or 1.5/1.5) for at least **24 m** rural or **18 m** urban.
- Exclusive turn lanes use WM2 instead of GM1. Mark the RM8 arrows in them.

**Other junction parts ([V2] 2.3.5.4):**
- The left edge line is dropped across the side-road mouth. A WM2 replaces it.
- Right-turn lanes are protected by an RM5 painted island, or by a WM2 on the same line.

## 5. Dual carriageways and freeways

**Class A1 freeway, per carriageway, left to right ([V2] 2.5.2.2; [V1] 7.2.8.6):**
1. RM4.1 left edge line, yellow, solid.
2. GM1 lane lines, white, broken, 2 m line and 4 m gap. Use the 12 m module, even in towns.
3. RM4.2 right edge line, **white**, solid. It is required on all freeway carriageways, with or without a barrier. Red studs go with it.

**Other dual carriageways:**
- Use the same layout.
- The right edge line is warranted where the median is not a barrier or kerbed island ([V1] 7.1.9.3(b)).

**Undivided freeway (Class A2) ([V2] 2.5.2.4–5):**
- Two lanes: mark it like a rural 2-lane road. Use RM2 through interchanges.
- Four lanes: a continuous RM5 painted median (recommended) or an RM2.

**Off-ramp ([V1] 7.2.8.10; [V2] 2.5.7):**
- The left edge line turns and becomes the ramp's left edge line.
- Across the ramp mouth, mark a WM2 continuity line, 300 mm wide on freeways.
- Mark the gore with an RM5 painted island in **chevrons**. They point forward, because traffic goes the same way on both sides.
- An RM3 next to the GM1, before the exit, is recommended. It stops late lane changes into the exit (Detail 2.70.1).

**On-ramp ([V1] 7.1.13.3, 7.2.8.10):**
- A tangential on-ramp needs no painted island.
- A loop ramp (180–360°) may get an RM5 in the gore.
- The main-road edge line restarts as the gore edge. It runs to a point 600 mm right of the ramp's WM2, then stops until it meets the ramp's edge line.

**Lane drops ([V1] 7.3.6; [V2] 2.5.6):**
- End the lane line before the taper.
- Mark 3 sets of WM6 arrows.
- Shade the dropped side with RM5.

> **Unconfirmed:** the manual says the right edge line is white. I did not confirm this against N2 photos. Some roads may show a yellow line on the median side.

## 6. Painted islands and medians (RM5)

**Rules ([V1] 7.2.9; [V4] 12.2.2, Fig 12.1–12.9):**

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Border | White solid line, 100 min. On one-way roads (and freeways), one border may be the yellow left edge line. One border may be a kerb. |
| Bars | **Yellow**. 150 mm to 1 m wide, measured square to the bar. [V2] Table 2.3 gives 200 rural and 150 urban. |
| Bar spacing | Bar to space = **1 to 2**, measured along the island centre line. |
| Angle | **30°** to the centre line or edge line. |
| Diagonal bars | Use them when traffic passes on one side only, or in **opposite** directions on each side. Bars slope forward in the direction of travel. |
| Chevrons | Use them when traffic goes the **same** way on both sides. The included angle is 60°, and they point forward. |
| Bar ends | Leave a 150 mm gap between bar ends and the border. |
| Wide bars | From 600 mm to 1 m, bars may be hollow boxes. For a 1 m bar: two 300 mm lines with a 400 mm gap. |
| First bar | On the leading end, make it 3–5 times longer for impact. On the trailing end, 2–3 times ([V4] 12.2.2.8). |
| Narrow median | Two white RM1 lines with a **solid yellow line** between them, 200–600 mm wide. Leave 50 mm clear (150 mm with studs). From 600 mm wide, use bars instead ([V1] 7.2.9.2(c); [V4] Fig 12.1 note 4). |
| Meaning | You may not drive on it, stop on it or park on it. The exceptions are an emergency or a traffic officer's order. |

**Common uses ([V1] 7.2.9.6):**
- Before a refuge island or a median island starts.
- To protect right-turn lanes.
- In freeway gores.
- As a separator between opposing flows where there is no room for a kerbed median.
- As a "shadow" beside a kerbed island.

**N2 / Beacon Way, Plettenberg Bay:**
- I could not confirm the actual layout there (**unconfirmed**). Going by the rules, a painted median between opposing N2 flows would use **diagonal** yellow bars at 30°, with white borders. Where it shelters a right-turn lane, it is an RM5 that widens with the turn-lane taper.
- Use the narrow-median form (white-yellow-white) where it is under 600 mm wide.
- Check this against aerial imagery before we build it.

## Algorithm notes: what each marking needs from our data

| Marking | Inputs needed |
|---|---|
| Module choice (9 m or 12 m) | Urban/rural flag (from landuse or place), `maxspeed` (80 or more gives 12 m), motorway class (always 12 m) |
| WM3 dividing line | Two-way road, surfaced, carriageway width (5.5 m or more must have it), lane count (multi-lane gives 150 mm and 1:1) |
| RM1 / RM2 zones | Centreline in 3D plus the road edge and shoulder break line (for horizontal sight), design speed (from `maxspeed` or class), direction. Sweep each direction with eye 1.05 m and object 1.30 m against the BSD table. Then join gaps under 120 m, stretch zones to the minimum length (150 m rolling, 60 m mountainous), and snap to modules. |
| WM8 arrows | Start point of each RM1 zone, per direction, and the module (24/60/108 m or 18/45/81 m) |
| RM2 vs RM1 | Access density (driveways or side roads less than 200 m apart rule out RM2), class |
| RM4.1 left edge line | Surfaced shoulder present (`shoulder`, `shoulder:width`), urban kerb or not, side-road mouths (for the gap and WM2) |
| RM4.2 right edge line | Dual carriageway or `oneway` part of a divided road, median type (barrier or kerb or none), urban/rural |
| GM1 lane lines | `lanes` per direction, carriageway width per direction (6.8 m or more, lanes 2.75 m or more), `turn:lanes` |
| RM3 stacking / channelising | Junction position on multi-lane approaches, exit and turn lanes, speed (300 mm above 80 km/h) |
| RTM1 / RTM2 | Junction node, which approach is minor, `highway=stop` or `give_way` nodes, traffic signals, main-road edge position (to place the line 1.2–15 m back), sight-distance test (Fig 2.13) if no sign is tagged |
| RM8 / WM7 arrows | `turn:lanes`, lane centre lines, stop-line position, distance to the previous side road |
| RTM3 / RTM4 / RM11 | `highway=crossing` and `crossing=*` (signals or zebra), position, lane count |
| RM5 painted islands | Gores where ways split or merge (same direction gives chevrons), median gaps between opposing flows (diagonal bars), turn-lane tapers, island width (under 600 mm gives the white-yellow-white form) |
| Studs | Road class (all freeways), line colour, urban/rural spacing |
| Tapers and lane drops | Speed, lateral shift (taper 1 in 20 to 1 in 50), lane count changes along the way |
